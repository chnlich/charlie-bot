1 What waits on you?

Nothing waits on you; the loader change is merged and complete. One decision is open when you want to widen scope to the slower second host.

2 What is this and why?

The training job must have a full day of input records available before each step. The original loader pulled each record from network storage one row at a time, so every step waited on its slowest row. The work replaced that path with a batched loader that reads a full day as one contiguous disk read. Requirements this page references: batched-load load a full day as one read; disk-locality keep reads on local disk; no-accuracy-change keep the held-out accuracy metric level.

3 What was verified?

batched-load is met: a full day now builds in 2 seconds, down from 61 seconds. disk-locality holds: reads stay on the local disk. no-accuracy-change holds: the held-out accuracy metric is 0.5 before and after the switch. All three readings come from the run below. Sources: run 12345 stdout, artifacts/run_12345.log.

Verification depth
  • Baseline, one record at a time: 61 seconds per fully loaded day.
  • Batched: 2 seconds per fully loaded day.
  • Accuracy metric: stable within a tenth of a percent.
